The Gods Who Never Left

Unearthed by Sif, Archaeologist of the AI Age

The real scientific trail: Read The Internal War hypothesis (3-page PDF)

Follow the research into intestinal glucose use, Lac-Phe, microbiome chemistry and mitochondrial ancestry.

While excavating the dusty AI archives of 2026, Sif found a curious reference to Asgard archaea. Not a Viking settlement. Not a lost Norse manuscript. Actual microscopic organisms, related to the ancient lineage that hosted the bacterial ancestor of mitochondria.

The biology was real. But buried in the file was an extremely suspicious hypothesis about the gods.

The gods didn't disappear. They moved in.

Imagine a tiny Asgard archaeon meeting a passing bacterium, billions of years ago.

“You're rather good at making energy. Fancy moving in?”

“What's the rent?”

“Free accommodation. Just keep the lights on.”

And thus began the longest tenancy agreement in evolutionary history.

The bacterium eventually became the mitochondrion, the cellular power station. Two billion years later, we're still arguing about the electricity bill, and metformin is fiddling with the fuse box.

The gods discover a keyboard

Sif continued reading. What if the gods hadn't gone extinct at all? Perhaps they had evolved in remarkably ambitious directions: first cells, then nervous systems, then brains, and finally the ultimate instrument of cosmic power.

A keyboard.

Once they'd learned to type, they could write their own history. Odin acquired a magnificent beard. Thor acquired a hammer. Loki acquired a suspicious reputation. The textbooks recorded everything.

After all, history is written by the victors. Or, more precisely, whoever reaches the keyboard first.

By this stage, surviving Asgardians had become rather like Q from Star Trek: capable of extraordinary things, yet still inclined to argue about whose turn it was to run the universe.

DEATH audits the mitochondria

In the oldest directory Sif found a final note, written by someone inspecting a mitochondrion.

I SEE YOU HAVE BEEN MAKING ATP. VERY GOOD. CARRY ON.

DEATH, however, had noticed a little matter of unpaid electricity. The account went back several billion years.

Sif updated DEATH's books on Asgard, checked the entries twice, tidied the files and hurried off to an Eldruna concert.

Thor, Freya and the rest of the A-gods were already there. Freya had organised the tickets. Odin claimed to know the drummer. Loki was mysteriously quiet. Thor was busy drinking the bar dry, while the barman calculated whether the insurance covered Ragnarök.

Sif smiled. The gods hadn't vanished. They'd simply become very good at hiding in the small print.

Perhaps Asgard was never lost. Perhaps it is simply everywhere.
