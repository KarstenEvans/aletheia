## The identity

**Our own imaginative Norse and Elvish-flavoured setting, with a touch of humour.**

Aletheia's World Tree draws on Yggdrasil and woodland folklore, but must not recreate Middle-earth, Discworld, Doctor Who, Star Wars, Star Trek, Galaxy Quest, Hitchhiker's Guide, Dragonriders of Pern or any other copyrighted fictional universe. Those worlds can be discussed, linked to or referred to in attributed commentary; they are **not** the setting, source code, trademark identity or canon of Aletheia. Our project can celebrate influences without copying their distinctive designs, dialogue or storylines.

Aletheia's two-tree green symbol in `assets/world-tree.svg` stays the primary emblem. The visual subtitle remains **The World Tree of Knowledge**; the main public description is **Aletheia, the Worldwide AI site**. The tree **connects rather than controls**.

## What visitors should feel

- An inviting evergreen, elvish-tinged landscape, Norse knots or subtle mythical motifs, softly illuminated golden threads and distant stars, without sacrificing readable controls.
- Wit that is warm, dry, curious and unforced, rather than a constant parody.
- Each knowledge branch has its own clear purpose. The main hub stays small, independent of any one AI company and easy to navigate.
- Evidence, correction and uncertainty matter. The mythic metaphor is **not** a scientific or historical authority.

## Norse weekday rabbit hole: preserve the language distinction

In English, **Saturday** comes from the Roman **Saturn**. In Scandinavian languages, Norwegian **lørdag** (Nynorsk **laurdag**) descends from Old Norse **laugardagr**, literally **bath/washing day**: **laug** means bath and **dagr** means day. That does **not** make Saturday 'Loki's day', and `dag` means **day**, not **dog**.

Other English weekday names include Tiw/Týr's day (Tuesday), Woden/Odin's day (Wednesday) and Thunor/Thor's day (Thursday), with Sun and Moon for Sunday and Monday. Friday has the Frigg/Freyja name question; do not invent Baldr as Tuesday.

References:
- [Viking Ship Museum, Roskilde: The names of the weekdays](https://www.vikingeskibsmuseet.dk/en/professions/education/viking-age-people/the-names-of-the-weekdays?L=1)
- [Etymonline: Saturday](https://www.etymonline.com/word/Saturday)

## Writing guardrails

1. Give practical content and working links priority over elaborate lore.
2. Build original stories and original designs. Allow literature, films and TV as *references*, not copied universes.
3. Distinguish **Norse source tradition**, **modern factual claim**, **creative embellishment**, **satire**, and **unproven theory**.
4. Maintain the exact World Tree motto and the green two-tree emblem.
5. Introduce new characters gradually and preserve only approved details in this source file.
6. Keep mystery and myth out of the way when people need evidence or accessibility.

**Potential first scene (not approved as canon):** Sif hosts a gathering beneath the World Tree where people offer dots of knowledge. A cat asks whether the dots have been checked. Even the dragon has to provide citations.
